# Vehicle bible

## Design rule

No real brands, logos, model names, or one-to-one distinctive silhouettes. Each vehicle blends broad cues from several archetypes and receives a fictional manufacturer, model, biography, and faction history.

## Archetypes

| Archetype | Strength | Weakness | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brawler | mass, armor, ramming | turning, repair cost | destroying rivals |
| Runner | acceleration, agility | armor, stability | shortcuts and escapes |
| Hauler | durability, debris handling | speed | convoy and off-road events |
| Rocket | speed, boost potential | fragile components | clean routes and time trials |
| Utility | cargo, support, stable platform | agility | deliveries and faction jobs |

## Required data

Every vehicle Data Asset must define identity, mass, engine, torque curve, top speed, steering, brakes, grip, suspension, armor, component health, repair cost, tuning slots, collision profile, visual damage parts, audio profile, faction associations, and unlock conditions.

## Tuning identity

Upgrades must create trade-offs rather than pure vertical power. Engine upgrades increase heat or damage risk; armor increases mass; grip can reduce durability; ramming parts affect frontal protection and handling.

## AI parity

AI vehicles use the same vehicle and tuning data as the player. Any AI-only exception must be documented and justified for readability or performance.
