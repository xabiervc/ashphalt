# Vision and pillars

## Product promise

Ashphalt is a chaotic but readable post-apocalyptic driving game where every race is simultaneously a contest, a fight, a repair problem, and a political decision.

## Target experience

The player should feel clever for using weight, damage, shortcuts, traffic, weather, faction relationships, and vehicle preparation together. The game is arcade-first, not a realistic driving simulator.

## Pillars

1. **Aggressive driving is useful.** Ramming, destruction, and calculated risk create opportunities.
2. **Vehicles have identity.** Every archetype has meaningful strengths and weaknesses.
3. **The world reacts.** Factions, patrols, workshops, routes, and story respond to the player's behavior.
4. **Preparation matters without becoming chores.** Repair and tuning are fast, visual, and strategically relevant.
5. **Chaos remains readable.** Damage, objectives, threats, rewards, and consequences are communicated clearly.
6. **Systems are testable.** Deterministic decisions, data-driven content, and explicit acceptance criteria reduce hidden complexity.

## Anti-pillars

- Not a realistic terrorism or military simulator.
- Not a grind-heavy live service.
- Not a collection of unconnected minigames.
- Not a story with dozens of meaningless binary dialogue choices.
- Not a multiplayer-first project.

## Scope priority

The first vertical slice must prove vehicle handling, collisions, damage readability, one district, one race, one rival, one workshop, and one visible faction consequence. Everything else is secondary until that slice is fun.
