# Award-caliber quality bar

## Purpose

Ashphalt should be developed to a standard capable of competing for recognition in categories represented by The Game Awards, D.I.C.E. Awards, BAFTA Games Awards, and Game Developers Choice Awards. This is an aspiration and a set of measurable requirements, not a prediction of nominations or wins.

## Quality pillars

- **Distinctive design:** the game must have a recognizable identity beyond being a destruction-racing homage.
- **Excellent feel:** steering, impacts, damage, audio, camera, and feedback must feel intentional and responsive.
- **Meaningful agency:** choices change systems, relationships, access, and endings in ways players can understand.
- **Cohesive presentation:** art, sound, UI, writing, mechanics, and world communicate the same tone.
- **Respect for players:** accessible controls, readable consequences, fair difficulty, recovery from failure, and no unnecessary grind.
- **Technical trust:** stable performance, fast recovery, robust saves, clear error states, and disciplined QA.

## Non-negotiable evidence

No feature is considered high quality because it sounds good in documentation. It must have:

1. A player-facing purpose.
2. A measurable acceptance criterion.
3. A test scenario.
4. A playtest result.
5. A known-risk decision.
6. A documented polish pass.

## Award-caliber vertical slice

The vertical slice must contain one district with a complete experience: preparation, driving, combat/destruction, rival pressure, weather or world event, workshop recovery, faction decision, consequence, narrative beat, audio feedback, accessibility options, save/load, and a polished ending screen.

The slice must be memorable after a single session without requiring the player to read the GDD.

## Quality questions

- What can only happen in Ashphalt?
- Can a player explain why they won or lost?
- Does destruction create decisions rather than noise?
- Do consequences appear at useful times and remain legible?
- Does the game respect players who do not want contact with pedestrians?
- Does every visual and audio effect improve understanding or emotion?
- Does the game remain fun when the player fails?
